## Simulated

| What | Stand-in | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Job descriptions, interviews, people | Generated by `scripts/make_data.py`; interviews written to show the method | Real ads are messier. The same author wrote paraphrases and taxonomy definitions, which helps the hashing embedder (it rewards shared words). |
| Gold labels | Written by the generator alongside the text | Independent of the pipeline, but not of the author's view of what a skill is. Real labels need two annotators and an agreement score. |
| Business numbers (45 minutes per profile, 1,400 roles, 10 minutes per review) | Assumptions | Labelled as such everywhere; the pilot design measures them. |
| CVs and their personal data | Generated; the masking patterns were written for this generator | 100% masking on generated CVs says little about real ones. Real CVs (tables, photos, LinkedIn exports, other formats for dates and phones) need a labelled sample and a masking recall target before the flag goes on. |
| Verifier cost | Estimated tokens (4 characters per token, 300 thinking tokens assumed) × list price | The first live run replaces the estimate with `usage` from the responses. |

## Bugs and design problems the evals caught during the build

1. **Fairness, invariance 60% → 100%.** He/she sentences, contact names and age-coded wording moved other skills' scores through weak semantic matches. Unit tests were green. Fixed by three rules ([ADR-0009](docs/03-decisions/ADR-0009-personal-traits-are-never-evidence.md)).
2. **A useless review band.** The first threshold rule (95% recall with review) put 2.1 items per profile in the band, 5.7% of them real. Replaced by a band-precision rule ([ADR-0003](docs/03-decisions/ADR-0003-calibrated-score-and-review-band.md)).
3. **Company talk without headings.** "All our plants are ISO 9001 certified" became a required skill when an ad had no section headings. Fixed with sentence cues (offer words, "we/our" voice) for descriptions without headings.
4. **Negation window too short for CVs.** "No hands-on experience with X yet" put the cue five tokens before the skill; the window was four. Widened to five, with tests in both languages.
5. **Double-barrelled names survived masking.** "Ann Peeters-Claes" did not match the name pattern; found by a hand-written test, not by the generated data (which has no double names). A reminder that generated data only tests what its author thought of.
6. **Negation scope.** "Python experience and no fear of change" was read as negated. After an alias, only a predicate ("is not needed", "geen vereiste") negates now (`tests/test_text.py`).
