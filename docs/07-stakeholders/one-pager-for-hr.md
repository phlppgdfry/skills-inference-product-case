# SkillDraft for HR: one page

**What it does.** You paste a job description. SkillDraft proposes the skills the role needs, marks each as *required* or *nice to have*, and highlights the sentence it came from. You accept, reject or add with one click. You decide; it drafts.

**What it does not do.** It does not judge people, rank candidates or decide anything. It does not keep your job descriptions.

**How good is it?** On our test set of <!--n:test.descriptions-->100<!--/n--> job descriptions in Dutch and English:
- of the skills it suggests, <!--n:D.precision-->0.963<!--/n--> are right;
- it finds <!--n:D.recall-->0.953<!--/n--> of the skills that are there, including skills that are only described ("you keep the lines running") and never named;
- it does not suggest skills just because your company mentions them ("our plants run on SAP") or because the ad says they are *not* needed;
- the profile does not change when the ad says "he" or "she", names a different contact person, or uses age-coded wording.

These numbers come from realistic test texts, not yet from Haldane's own ads. The first pilot step checks them on 40 of yours.

**What you will notice.** A few skills per profile may say *please check*: the system is unsure and asks you. Sentences it could not link to any skill are listed, so you can see what it missed. And it flags wording like "young and dynamic team" with a neutral alternative.

**What we ask of you in the pilot.** Two HR partners, four weeks, about 20 profiles each, and honest comments. We measure the time per profile before and after.

**The expected gain (assumption until the pilot).** Writing a profile takes about 45 minutes today. If reviewing a draft takes 10, the 1,400 Haldane roles need about 230 hours instead of about 1,050.
